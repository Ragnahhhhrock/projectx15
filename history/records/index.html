Mach 6.7 · 354,200 ft

X-15 speed and altitude records

The X-15 holds two famous marks: Mach 6.7 and 354,200 ft. NASA's fact sheet lists both as unofficial, and the speed is widely cited as the fastest ever recorded by a crewed, powered aircraft.

The speed record

The standard X-15 reached Mach 6.04 on 9 November 1961, flown by Robert White. Mach 6.7 needed the rebuilt X-15A-2, with its longer fuselage, external tanks and ablative coating. Pete Knight flew it on 3 October 1967. The aircraft was carrying a dummy ramjet engine on a pylon and came back with heat damage. Walker's fastest flight was flight 59 on 27 June 1962, at 4,104 mph.

The altitude record

Joe Walker flew 354,200 ft on 22 August 1963, a month after flight 90 reached 347,800 ft. Those are the only two X-15 flights above 328,000 ft (100 km), the line the international aeronautics federation (FAI) uses for space.

Astronaut wings: the 50-mile line

The US Air Force recognised 50 miles (264,000 ft) as the edge of space. Thirteen X-15 flights went above it, by eight pilots: Dana, Engle, Knight, McKay, Rushworth, Walker, White and Adams. The five Air Force pilots already held astronaut wings; Michael Adams, killed on flight 191 at 266,000 ft, received his posthumously. In 2005 NASA gave its own wings to Dana, McKay (posthumously) and Walker.
